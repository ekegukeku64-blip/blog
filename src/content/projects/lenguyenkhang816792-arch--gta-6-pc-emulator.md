## Installation Walkthrough

So you've decided to bring Leonida to your desktop. Good call. Before the neon hits your screen, let's get your rig primed. This walkthrough assumes you're on Windows 10 or 11 and that you're the type who reads the fine print before clicking "next" — which, around here, is a virtue.

**Step 1 — Prep the Canvas**

Update Windows fully, then restart. Yes, actually restart. Then visit your GPU vendor's official driver utility and pull the latest package. A fresh driver is the closest thing to a tune-up your machine will ever ask for.

**Step 2 — Make Room for Vice City**

GTA VI will be a heavyweight. Carve out generous SSD space and verify it's healthy before you begin. Treat your install drive like a garage — clear the clutter first, then park something beautiful inside.

**Step 3 — Stage the Companion Utility**

The GTA 6 PC Emulator installer is your setup companion, not the game itself. Run the installer, follow the prompts, and let it review your Windows environment — DirectX status, driver, storage headroom, and hardware profile. Think of it as a pre-flight checklist: it tells you what needs attention before the real journey starts.

**Step 4 — Tend to the Rockstar Games Launcher**

Prepare the official Rockstar Games Launcher on your system and keep it updated. That's the doorway through which the actual game will eventually arrive — this project simply makes sure the hallway is swept and the lights are on.

**Step 5 — Tune Windows for Play**

Enable Game Mode, confirm your monitor's refresh rate, and make sure Windows is pointing at the right GPU if you're running more than one. Keep airflow healthy and background processes lean. Small courtesies now pay dividends in frame-time later.

A final note, modder to modder: this companion tool smooths the road, but it doesn't smuggle the destination. The game itself must come through an authorized distributor when the PC edition lands. Until then, keep your drivers sharp and your SSD hungry — Vice City rewards the prepared.
